## Summary

One paragraph, in plain language, that a reader can act on without reading further.
What is being proposed, and what changes if it is accepted.

## Motivation

Why this is needed. Describe the problem, not the solution: what goes wrong today,
who it affects, and what cannot be done. If nothing is broken, say what would become
possible and why that matters.

## Guide-level explanation

Explain the change as if to someone who will use it. Concrete examples, real commands,
and the error messages a person would see. This is the section reviewers who are not
implementing it will read.

## Reference-level explanation

The details: data shapes, protocol behaviour, configuration keys, migration steps,
compatibility, and anything else needed to implement it unambiguously. Name the files.

## Drawbacks

What gets worse. Every proposal has a cost; state it rather than letting a reviewer
find it.

## Rationale and alternatives

Why this shape rather than the obvious alternatives. What was rejected, and why.
Include the option of doing nothing.

## Prior art

How other projects solve this, with links. Read them rather than citing them from
memory.

## Unresolved questions

What must be settled before acceptance, and by whom.

## Future possibilities

What this deliberately leaves out, and what it makes easier later.

## Adoption plan

"done when" test is for each.
